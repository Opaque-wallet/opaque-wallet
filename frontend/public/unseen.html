    How a read leaves your browser

    Four steps. None of them trust us.

    01 · PINNED

    The code is pinned on chain.

    The wallet reads the Nox worker's bundle hash from its IWorkerSpecifier contract and runs only bytes that match it. Nobody can quietly swap the code.

    02 · SANDBOXED

    It runs where it can't see you.

    A Web Worker inside a sandboxed, null-origin iframe. It can't reach the page, its storage, or your keys. All it gets is the request.

    03 · THREE HOPS

    Wrapped in layers, mixed on the way.

    Each request becomes Sphinx packets. Each Nox node peels one layer, waits, and passes it on among everyone else's. TLS to the RPC runs inside the worker, so the exit relays bytes it can't read.

    04 · ANSWERED

    The chain answers the mixnet.

    Balances, your key's state, and your account's operations to the public bundler: all answered, none tied to you. That's about a second a read.

    Honest about it

    What it doesn't hide.

    Built on the Ethereum Foundation's anon-rpc and the Nox mixnet. Opaque's code is open source (Apache-2.0). On Sepolia today.

    • One bootstrap read. The wallet reads the worker's hash from its contract once, directly, before anything is anonymized.
    • Payments still use Opaque's relays. A private payment needs our attester, so it crosses our three-hop mesh, which hides your IP but is one operator.
    • Slower. Mixing is the point: a read takes about a second instead of a blink.
    • The chain is still public. anon-rpc hides who asks. Opaque's pools and stealth addresses hide who pays and who is paid.
