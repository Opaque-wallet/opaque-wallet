    How your notes stay private

    Your coins sit in a pool among notes of the same size. A payment proves it owns one of 8 of them without saying which, and the pool pays from its own account.

    The pool's keys sign once each, and every payout swaps in keys nobody has seen yet. That protects against a future quantum computer by timing: an attacker gets a few seconds per key. It is not a mathematical guarantee.

    How a private send works

    1. Whole notesYour balance is notes of 1, 2, 5, 10, 20, 50 and up, to 1,000,000, split so any whole amount can be paid. A send spends the fewest that add up to it exactly.
    2. A ring of eightEach note is spent by a proof that it is one of 8 in the pool, never which.
    3. The pool paysThe recipient is paid from the pool's own account. Your address never appears on the ledger.

    How a private swap works

    1. Spent through a ringYour notes are spent by a proof that each is one of 8 in its pool, never which.
    2. Converted by the ledgerThe pool pays through the XRP Ledger's own AMM. Nobody else holds your funds on the way.
    3. Lands as new notesWhat it buys arrives in a fresh tag of the other pool, so nobody can link the two. No key of yours signs: the proof does.
